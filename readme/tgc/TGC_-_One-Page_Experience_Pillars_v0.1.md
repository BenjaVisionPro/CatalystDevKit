# TGC — One-Page Experience Pillars v0.1

## Core Experience

**That Game: Catalyst** is built around one foundational experience:

> **Players feel present with each other in a world they can understand, shape, and care about.**

The experience pillars define how TGC should feel, how systems should be judged, and what production decisions should protect.

---

## 1. Human Presence First

TGC is a multiplayer social world before it is a combat game, crafting game, building game, survival game, or story game.

Players should feel that other people are genuinely present in the same environment.

**Decision Rule:**  
A system is stronger when it helps players notice, understand, cooperate with, or respond to each other naturally.

---

## 2. Clean Communication

Communication is core infrastructure.

Proximity audio, directional sound, group channels, environmental acoustics, and communication context are foundational systems.

**Decision Rule:**  
Every communication feature should increase clarity, comfort, presence, or social agency.

---

## 3. Seamless Collaboration

Cooperation should feel easy without being mandatory.

Players should move naturally between solo play, small-group play, public-space interaction, faction activity, settlement work, and organised missions.

**Decision Rule:**  
When players want to do something together, the game should help them coordinate, contribute, and understand progress.

---

## 4. Comfortable Complexity

TGC contains deep systems, and those systems must feel approachable.

The game includes building, crafting, farming, economy, power, automation, factions, governance, combat, exploration, terraforming, transport, AI, wildlife, and long-term progression.

**Decision Rule:**  
Every deep system needs a simple entry point, clear feedback, and a reason to exist in shared play.

---

## 5. Player-Shaped Worlds

Players should leave visible, persistent, meaningful marks on the world.

TGC is about participating in frontier societies and shaping the places where social life happens.

**Decision Rule:**  
Player effort should produce visible change, useful infrastructure, social value, or lasting consequence.

---

## 6. Systems That Create Reasons to Interact

TGC’s systems should create social gravity.

The game should give players practical reasons to seek each other out, talk, trade, specialise, negotiate, travel, ask for help, offer help, form organisations, or disagree.

**Decision Rule:**  
A system should create opportunities for useful, interesting, or memorable player interaction.

---

## 7. Story Gives Meaning to Shared Action

Narrative supports the sandbox.

Story explains why the world matters, why players gather, why factions disagree, why ruins are dangerous, why VERITAS cannot be fully trusted, and why building civilisation is more than resource management.

**Decision Rule:**  
Narrative should create reasons to explore, build, cooperate, investigate, debate, and care.

---

## Stakeholder Takeaway

TGC is not defined by the number of systems it contains.

It is defined by how those systems combine to produce a specific feeling:

> **A comfortable, expressive, socially alive frontier where players communicate naturally, shape the world together, and create shared history.**

That feeling is the product.
