# TGC — Vertical Slice Brief v0.2

## 1. Vertical Slice Purpose

The vertical slice proves the foundation of **That Game: Catalyst**.

It does not prove the full feature set. It proves the core experience:

> **TGC feels like a socially alive multiplayer world worth building on.**

The slice should demonstrate that players can enter a shared space, communicate naturally, cooperate on a practical task, explore a nearby mystery, change the local world state, and leave wanting to continue.

## 2. Experience Goal

The vertical slice should make the player understand:

- I am physically present in a shared world.
- Other players are readable, audible, and socially approachable.
- Communication feels clean and spatial.
- The world invites cooperation without forcing it.
- Building and environment changes matter.
- Exploration reveals useful and mysterious information.
- VERITAS or Harmony helps me understand the world.
- Something beneath the official story is unresolved.
- Other players make the experience better.

## 3. Slice Summary

The recommended vertical slice is a contained frontier settlement and nearby alien point of interest. Players begin in or near a small settlement on Nova Terra.

The settlement includes a social gathering area, basic movement/traversal, clean proximity and directional voice, simple group communication, a repair/build area, simple resource collection, simple crafting/construction, shared storage or infrastructure, VERITAS or Harmony onboarding, a local mission or guidance surface, a nearby alien ruin or anomaly, and a local faction pressure or settlement problem.

The slice supports a small multiplayer session, ideally **2–8 players**.

The experience flow: arrival, orientation, social communication, simple preparation, repair/construction, exploration, discovery, light challenge, return, visible world change, unresolved mystery.

## 4. Recommended Scenario — The Relay Beneath the Ridge

A small Nova Terra settlement is experiencing intermittent power and communication failures.

VERITAS attributes the issue to environmental instability. Harmony asks the players to restore local systems and investigate inconsistent readings near a nearby ridge.

Player flow:

1. Players arrive in the settlement.
2. Harmony introduces movement, communication, and basic interaction.
3. Players locate and understand each other using proximity audio.
4. VERITAS identifies a damaged relay or power node.
5. Players gather basic resources nearby.
6. Players repair or build the relay component together.
7. The relay partially restores settlement function.
8. The restored system reveals anomalous readings from the ridge.
9. Players travel to the point of interest.
10. They discover a partially buried alien structure.
11. A scan produces data VERITAS cannot fully reconcile.
12. A light hazard, puzzle, or hostile system response occurs.
13. Players recover an artefact fragment or corrupted record.
14. They return to the settlement.
15. The settlement gains a visible improvement.
16. VERITAS revises the mission record or Harmony expresses concern.
17. A new unresolved question is introduced.

The settlement is measurably improved, the players have worked together, and the world feels larger, stranger, and more socially alive than it did at the start.

## 5. Required Experience Proofs

### Social Presence

Players must feel that other players are physically present in the same place. The slice proves spatial positioning, body presence, proximity awareness, directional audio, distance falloff, smooth local conversation, and comfort when multiple people are nearby.

### Communication Contexts

Players must understand when they are speaking locally, privately, in a group, or through a mediated channel. The slice proves local proximity voice, small group communication, clear channel state, readable speaker identity, basic mute/block/comfort control, and transition between local and group contexts.

### Comfortable Social Space

The settlement should feel like a place, not a spawn room. The slice proves readable layout, safe social space, useful staging area, clear exits/landmarks, environmental identity, gathering spaces without gameplay pressure, and calm where appropriate.

### Shared Building or Repair

The slice needs a simple construction loop showing how players shape the world together. The recommended objective is to repair or build a small settlement structure that enables the next step of play.

### Shared Exploration and Discovery

The slice should include a nearby point of interest that players can approach, investigate, and discuss: a partially exposed alien structure near the settlement that appears dormant but reacts to player presence or VERITAS analysis.

### VERITAS or Harmony Guidance

The slice should introduce the AI layer as practical, useful, and slightly uncertain. Harmony introduces the settlement and basic systems. VERITAS provides mission data and analysis. A discovery causes VERITAS to hesitate, revise, omit, or contradict an earlier statement.

### Local Problem With Practical Purpose

The settlement has a failing infrastructure node linked to unexplained interference from a nearby alien structure.

### Meaningful World Change

The ending should create both satisfaction and curiosity: settlement power restored, communication tower online, scanner reveals hidden ruin layer, faction message intercepted, alien structure activates, new map marker appears, corrupted record unlocked, Harmony concerned, or the settlement gains a usable facility.

## 6. Required Systems

### Core Required

1. Multiplayer session support
2. Third-person player movement
3. Directional proximity voice
4. Basic group communication
5. Player identity and speaker readability
6. Basic social comfort controls
7. Settlement environment
8. Simple resource collection
9. Simple building, repair, or placement
10. Basic inventory interaction
11. Simple crafting or construction requirement
12. One guided mission or activity
13. One exploration point of interest
14. VERITAS or Harmony interaction
15. One mystery clue or contradiction
16. Visible world-state change

### Strongly Recommended

1. Environmental audio treatment
2. Basic permissions for shared construction or storage
3. Simple hostile or hazardous encounter
4. Basic faction presence
5. Simple scanning or analysis tool
6. Shared objective tracking
7. Return-to-settlement flow
8. Session persistence for built or repaired elements

### Optional for Slice

Vehicles, advanced crafting, advanced combat, economy, governance, large-scale terraforming, wildlife simulation, worker AI, complex faction reputation, multi-world travel, full character arcs, and deep alien mythology.

Optional does not mean unimportant. It means not required to prove the first experience.

## 7. Location Requirements

**Settlement Area:** arrival point, social gathering space, shelter/hub, repair/build site, shared storage or crafting station, visible power/communication infrastructure, path to wilderness, landmarks, calm audio profile.

**Wilderness Path:** readable route, minor traversal challenge, resource nodes, sightline to point of interest, environmental identity, and opportunity for conversation while moving.

**Alien Point of Interest:** distinct silhouette, scan/inspect target, small puzzle/hazard/encounter, artefact or clue, environmental audio shift, and enough space for multiple players to gather.

## 8. Narrative Requirements

The slice introduces narrative through action: humanity has settled this world, the settlement depends on maintained systems, VERITAS is the official mission source, Harmony/VERITAS helps practically, alien traces exist nearby, the official explanation is incomplete, player action reveals something new, and the world changes because of what players did.

Avoid long cutscenes, heavy exposition, deep Aethereon explanation, full faction history, lore dumps, too many characters, or explaining the full Veiled Directive.

## 9. Audio Requirements

Audio is the most important proof point. Players should identify speaker direction, hear distance naturally, have comfortable local conversation, keep group communication clear, experience calm settlement ambience, hear a distinct alien point-of-interest identity, and notice system changes without intrusive audio.

Audio success should be tested in real multiplayer sessions.

## 10. Explicitly Out of Scope

The vertical slice should not attempt to prove full economy, full faction simulation, full governance, full crafting professions, advanced automation, large-scale settlement management, multi-planet travel, stock market systems, succession/inheritance, complex wildlife ecology, large-scale terraforming, full PvP, full campaign, deep alien metaphysics, complete character arcs, large numbers of worlds, or endgame loops.

## 11. Success Criteria

The slice succeeds if players naturally talk in-world, understand where voices come from, gather comfortably, cooperate on a practical task, see their actions change the environment, discover something mysterious, understand VERITAS/Harmony as useful interfaces, feel social/narrative depth, and want to continue exploring or building after the objective ends.

The strongest success signal:

> **Players stay in the world after the objective is complete because being there with others feels good.**

## 12. Stakeholder Summary

The vertical slice should be small, polished, social, and memorable.

It should show why TGC is worth building:

> **A seamless multiplayer social world where communication feels natural, collaboration feels useful, the environment responds, and shared discovery gives players a reason to care.**
