# TGC — Systems Context Brief v0.2

## 1. System-Level Context

TGC’s major systems form a connected social-world architecture.

The game is a third-person multiplayer civilisation sandbox where players explore alien worlds, build settlements, gather resources, craft tools, operate vehicles, fight threats, join factions, trade, govern, terraform environments, discover ruins, and uncover hidden forces shaping human expansion.

These systems are not isolated feature buckets. They exist to create a world where players have reasons to meet, talk, cooperate, disagree, specialise, build, protect, discover, and remember what they have done together.

## 3. Social Communication

**Function:** Allows players to feel present with each other, understand social context, coordinate activity, and manage comfort.

**Includes:** proximity voice, directional multiplayer sound, distance falloff, group communication, private/public contexts, faction/mission/settlement channels, environmental acoustics, moderation/comfort controls, future translation, AI listening, altered speech, broadcasts, and diegetic communication devices.

**Social Value:** Communication is the floor everything else stands on. TGC is building a sandbox around embodied communication.

## 4. Movement and Embodiment

**Function:** Makes players feel physically present and able to act confidently in the world.

**Includes:** third-person traversal, walking, running, sprinting, dodging, climbing, parkour, jumping, ledge traversal, swimming, cover transitions, animation blending, momentum, physics-aware movement, and environmental interaction.

**Social Value:** Movement is part of social readability. Players read each other through position, distance, facing, hesitation, approach, retreat, and shared traversal.

## 5. Exploration and Discovery

**Function:** Gives players reasons to leave settlements, take risks, find opportunities, uncover story, and bring knowledge back to others.

**Includes:** open-world traversal, points of interest, alien ruins, hidden locations, resource surveying, environmental hazards, puzzle sites, planet/moon discovery, lore fragments, artefacts, route finding, and frontier expansion.

**Social Value:** Discovery creates stories players tell each other, not just icons they clear from a map.

## 6. Building and Settlement Creation

**Function:** Lets players create homes, workshops, bases, public spaces, defences, transport hubs, markets, farms, research sites, and faction infrastructure.

**Includes:** modular placement, building tools, crafted materials, fixtures, decoration, blueprints, collaborative construction, structural integrity, upgrades, maintenance, vertical building, and integration with power, storage, crafting, farming, automation, and defence.

**Social Value:** Building creates places where social life happens.

## 7. Resource Gathering and Crafting

**Function:** Allows players to survive, specialise, build, trade, prepare, experiment, and contribute to group goals.

**Includes:** mining, earthworks, farming, harvesting, material quality, resource rarity, modular crafting, recipe discovery, proficiency, mastery, professions, collaborative crafting, recycling, specialised equipment, environmental adaptation gear, and large multi-stage projects.

**Social Value:** Resources and crafting create healthy interdependence, specialisation, trade, production chains, settlement support, and reputation.

## 8. Infrastructure

**Function:** Allows settlements to operate, scale, defend themselves, and become socially useful places.

**Includes:** renewable power, fuel-based power, advanced power, grids, substations, storage, access control, automated storage/crafting/harvesting/processing, blueprint construction, defensive automation, maintenance bots, monitoring, disaster mitigation, and circuit design/programming.

**Social Value:** Infrastructure creates shared dependency and shared pride.

## 9. Economy, Trade, and Organisations

**Function:** Gives value to specialisation, distance, scarcity, reputation, production, risk, and trust.

**Includes:** player-owned shops, auctions, live trade events, player trading, crafting markets, scarcity, currency, taxes/regulations, consignment, trade organisations, settlements, alliances, exploration groups, research groups, and player-driven governments.

**Social Value:** Economy creates reasons for strangers to interact peacefully. A good economy makes social trust valuable.

## 10. Factions and Governance

**Function:** Creates social and political context for identity, cooperation, conflict, law, territory, and consequence.

**Includes:** faction reputation, ideological conflict, territory control, resource disputes, leadership, voting, laws, policy changes, succession, inheritance, faction missions, faction spaces, and faction rewards/restrictions.

**Social Value:** Factions are social pressure systems, not themed reward vendors.

## 11. Missions, Activities, and World Events

**Function:** Provides structured goals, urgency, challenge, rewards, social focus, and narrative momentum.

**Includes:** main/side/exploration/rescue/combat/stealth/puzzle missions, building objectives, contextual activities, seasonal activities, local/system/multi-world events, replayable challenges, and cooperative objectives.

**Social Value:** Missions and events organise attention inside the world.

## 12. Combat, Stealth, and Encounters

**Function:** Provides danger, protection, skill expression, tactical decision-making, and moments of consequence.

**Includes:** real-time combat, melee, ranged and energy weapons, non-lethal options, special devices, dodging, blocking/parrying, cover, stealth, bosses, enemy AI, environmental combat interactions, damage types/resistances, cooperative combat, and PvP where appropriate.

**Social Value:** Combat creates risk, protection, recovery, and high-intensity communication moments without dominating the identity of the game.

## 13. Vehicles and Transportation

**Function:** Moves people, goods, tools, animals, resources, and stories across large worlds.

**Includes:** personal/shared vehicles, mountable animals, trains, public transport, spacecraft, warp gates, player-owned transport, managed transport networks, vehicle crafting/modification/maintenance, racing, travel events, and transport companies.

**Social Value:** Transportation turns distance into logistics, trade, risk, infrastructure, and social planning.

## 14. AI Systems

**Function:** Makes the world more readable, alive, responsive, and useful.

**Includes:** VERITAS, Harmony, friendly NPCs, companions, enemy AI, worker AI, maintenance bots, wildlife AI, ecological behaviours, group tactics, navigation, dialogue, quest support, training, guidance, behaviour monitoring, and exploit prevention.

**Social Value:** AI supports the human social experience; it does not replace it.

## 15. Narrative, Artefacts, and Long-Form Mystery

**Function:** Gives meaning to discovery, conflict, technology, ruins, factions, and the frontier itself.

**Includes:** VERITAS, Harmony, the Veiled Directive, ancient ruins, alien artefacts, environmental storytelling, faction narratives, character relationships, mission clues, world events, and long-term mystery progression.

**Social Value:** Mystery creates shared curiosity.

## 16. Progression and Mastery

**Function:** Gives players growth, recognition, long-term goals, and ways to specialise.

**Includes:** experience levels, specialisation skill trees, fine tuning, item proficiency, expertise, crafting mastery, profession identity, loadouts, achievements, replayability, higher-difficulty rewards, mission mastery, and community challenges.

**Social Value:** Progression creates identity and contribution, not just bigger numbers.

## 17. Implementation Priority Lens

**Foundation Priority:** directional social audio, third-person movement, shared-world multiplayer interaction, basic building and settlement space, exploration and points of interest, simple resources/crafting, and VERITAS/Harmony.

**Expansion Priority:** faction reputation and missions, power/storage/infrastructure, vehicles and transport, economy and trade, world events, AI, terraforming and ecological impact.

**Long-Term Priority:** governance, advanced automation, large-scale economy, multi-world events, succession/inheritance, deep faction politics, complex terraforming, and long-form alien mystery.

## 18. Stakeholder Summary

Every system should strengthen the core experience:

> **A comfortable, expressive, socially alive frontier where players communicate naturally, shape the world together, and create shared history.**
