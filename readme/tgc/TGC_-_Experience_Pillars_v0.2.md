# TGC — Experience Pillars v0.2

## 1. Core Principle

TGC is built around one foundational experience:

> **Players feel present with each other in a world they can understand, shape, and care about.**

These pillars define how that experience is protected across design, architecture, audio, UI, multiplayer systems, world-building, narrative, and production. They are decision rules. A system that does not support them should be questioned, reduced, delayed, or removed.

## 2. Human Presence First

TGC is a multiplayer social world before it is a combat game, crafting game, building game, survival game, or story game. Players should feel that other people are genuinely present in the same environment.

**Design Rule:** A system is stronger when it helps players notice, understand, cooperate with, or respond to each other naturally.

**Failure Mode:** The world feels like a lobby, menu, or activity queue instead of a place people inhabit.

## 3. Clean Communication

Communication is core infrastructure. Proximity audio, directional sound, group channels, environmental acoustics, and communication context are foundational systems.

**Design Rule:** Every communication feature should increase clarity, comfort, presence, or social agency.

**Failure Mode:** Players rely on external voice tools because in-world communication is unclear, annoying, unsafe, or socially exhausting.

## 4. Seamless Collaboration

Cooperation should feel easy without being mandatory. Players should move naturally between solo play, small-group play, public-space interaction, faction activity, settlement work, and organised missions.

**Design Rule:** When players want to do something together, the game should help them coordinate, contribute, and understand progress.

**Failure Mode:** Players want to cooperate but spend most of their time fighting permissions, UI, unclear ownership, poor communication, or awkward party mechanics.

## 5. Comfortable Complexity

TGC contains deep systems, and those systems must feel approachable. The game includes building, crafting, farming, economy, power, automation, factions, governance, combat, exploration, terraforming, transport, AI, wildlife, and long-term progression.

**Design Rule:** Every deep system needs a simple entry point, clear feedback, and a reason to exist in shared play.

**Failure Mode:** The game becomes impressive on paper but exhausting in practice.

## 6. Player-Shaped Worlds

Players should leave visible, persistent, meaningful marks on the world. TGC is about participating in frontier societies and shaping the places where social life happens.

**Design Rule:** Player effort should produce visible change, useful infrastructure, social value, or lasting consequence.

**Failure Mode:** Players consume content but never feel they are changing the world.

## 7. Systems That Create Reasons to Interact

TGC’s systems should create social gravity. The game should give players practical reasons to seek each other out, talk, trade, specialise, negotiate, travel, ask for help, offer help, form organisations, or disagree.

**Design Rule:** A system should create opportunities for useful, interesting, or memorable player interaction.

**Failure Mode:** Players technically share a world but mostly ignore each other because the optimal path is solitary and silent.

## 8. Story Gives Meaning to Shared Action

Narrative in TGC exists to support the sandbox. Story explains why the world matters, why players gather, why factions disagree, why ruins are dangerous, why VERITAS cannot be fully trusted, and why building a civilisation is more than resource management.

**Design Rule:** Narrative should create reasons to explore, build, cooperate, investigate, debate, and care.

**Failure Mode:** Story becomes decorative lore wallpaper or a separate campaign that competes with the sandbox.

## 9. Applying the Pillars

When reviewing a feature, system, story beat, or technical decision, ask whether it improves human presence, communication, collaboration, approachable complexity, player-shaped worlds, player interaction, or story meaning.

## 10. Stakeholder Summary

TGC is not defined by the number of systems it contains. It is defined by how those systems combine to produce a specific feeling:

> **A comfortable, expressive, socially alive frontier where players communicate naturally, shape the world together, and create shared history.**
