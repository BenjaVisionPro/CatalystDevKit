# TGC — Stakeholder Overview v0.2

## 1. Executive Summary

**That Game: Catalyst** is a multiplayer social world built around presence, communication, shared agency, and player-shaped civilisation.

The project is expressed through a third-person sci-fi civilisation sandbox. Players explore alien worlds, build settlements, gather resources, craft tools, trade, govern, complete missions, join factions, uncover ruins, and investigate hidden forces shaping human expansion.

These systems are not separate genre features. They exist to create reasons for people to spend time together.

The foundation of TGC is human socialisation: clean directional multiplayer sound, proximity-aware communication, environmental audio, group coordination, comfortable public spaces, and seamless collaboration.

The intended result is a world that feels natural to inhabit with other people — a place where players can talk, build, explore, organise, disagree, recover, discover, and create shared history.

## 2. Core Proposition

TGC is not a sandbox with multiplayer features attached.

It is a social civilisation sandbox where communication, collaboration, and shared presence make every system more meaningful.

Players are pioneers in a living frontier. They do not simply complete content. They participate in the development of settlements, social structures, trade networks, faction conflicts, research efforts, infrastructure, and long-term discovery.

The project’s differentiation is not the size of its feature list.

The differentiation is the intended feeling of shared presence:

> **Players feel present with each other in a world they can understand, shape, and care about.**

## 3. The Social Foundation

TGC starts from a simple design belief:

> People enjoy games more when being with other people feels natural.

The architecture supports that from the ground up.

Players should be able to hear where others are, understand who is speaking, move naturally between local/group/private/public/mission/faction/settlement communication, join or leave social situations comfortably, coordinate without fighting the interface, gather in shared spaces that feel calm/readable/alive, and recognise other players as embodied people in the world.

This is more than voice chat. It is an embodied communication layer that supports social presence across the entire game.

## 4. The Player Experience

The intended player experience is seamless, comfortable, empowering, expressive, and socially alive.

- **Seamless:** Players move between exploration, building, conversation, missions, trade, travel, and combat without feeling like they are changing software modes.
- **Comfortable:** Social interaction feels manageable rather than overwhelming. Audio clarity, spatial separation, moderation, social controls, and player agency protect the experience.
- **Empowering:** Players can affect the world. They can build useful structures, form groups, solve local problems, claim land, improve settlements, discover new places, or shift faction outcomes.
- **Expressive:** Players show identity through architecture, organisation, tools, clothing, vehicles, settlement design, trade, diplomacy, reputation, and the roles they choose to play in society.
- **Socially Alive:** The world feels inhabited by people with goals, conflicts, habits, loyalties, needs, and relationships.

## 5. What Players Do

| Activity | Social Function |
|---|---|
| Exploration | Creates shared discovery, danger, wonder, and reasons to travel together. |
| Building | Creates persistent places where social life happens. |
| Crafting | Creates specialisation, trade, dependency, reputation, and identity. |
| Resource gathering | Creates cooperation, logistics, economy, scarcity, and territorial value. |
| Missions | Provide structured shared goals and narrative momentum. |
| Combat | Creates risk, protection needs, faction tension, and high-intensity coordination. |
| Factions | Turn ideology, resources, and power into social conflict. |
| Economy | Creates reasons to trade, specialise, organise, negotiate, and trust. |
| Governance | Allows communities to make decisions and live with consequences. |
| Terraforming | Lets players reshape worlds at civilisational scale. |
| Transport | Connects people, settlements, markets, resources, and frontiers. |
| Story discovery | Gives shared purpose to ruins, artefacts, AI anomalies, and exploration. |

These systems are social engines.

## 6. Communication as Core Infrastructure

Communication is one of TGC’s most important technical foundations. The communication layer includes proximity voice, clean directional sound, distance falloff, group communication, private/public contexts, settlement/faction/mission communication, environmental acoustics, comfort/moderation controls, and future support for translation, AI listening, altered speech, broadcasts, and diegetic communication devices.

Most multiplayer games treat communication as an external utility. TGC treats it as part of the world.

## 7. The Game World

TGC’s world supports long-term shared play: settlements that grow and change, public/private spaces, player-built infrastructure, faction-controlled regions, environmental risk, resource pressure, ancient ruins, social gathering places, transport networks, markets, conflict zones, and quiet places that feel good to inhabit.

Not every location is a combat arena. Some spaces exist as workshops, farms, outposts, observatories, markets, hangars, civic halls, transport hubs, research stations, ruins, lounges, and informal gathering places.

## 8. Narrative’s Role

Narrative supports the sandbox by giving meaning to shared action. Narrative systems include VERITAS, Harmony, the Veiled Directive, factions, alien artefacts, ancient infrastructure, characters, and world events.

The narrative should be discovered through play: exploration, faction conflict, settlement events, VERITAS analysis, environmental evidence, artefacts, missions, and contradictions between official records and physical reality.

## 9. What Makes TGC Different

TGC combines deep sandbox systems with a social architecture designed to make multiplayer presence feel natural, comfortable, and meaningful.

The product is not just what players can do. The product is how it feels to do those things with other people.

## 10. Stakeholder Takeaway

TGC is a socially grounded multiplayer world where clean communication, shared presence, player-shaped environments, and long-form discovery combine into a civilisation sandbox people want to inhabit together.

Everything builds from that centre.
