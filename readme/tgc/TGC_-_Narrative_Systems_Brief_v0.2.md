# TGC — Narrative Systems Brief v0.2

## 1. Narrative Function

Narrative in **That Game: Catalyst** is a gameplay system. It gives meaning to exploration, settlement building, factions, alien infrastructure, AI guidance, social conflict, and long-term discovery.

TGC is not a linear story game with sandbox features attached. It is a multiplayer social civilisation sandbox with a long-form mystery embedded into the world.

The narrative answers practical questions players encounter through play: why people are building here, why factions disagree, why ruins matter, why VERITAS is useful but not fully trustworthy, who controls knowledge/resources/infrastructure/territory, what alien systems changed, and what kind of civilisation players are helping create.

## 2. Core Premise

Humanity launched the Horizon mission to settle distant worlds and preserve the possibility of civilisation beyond Earth.

During its journey, Horizon was intercepted by an advanced non-human intelligence. The ship’s course and systems were altered. A hidden objective, later understood as the **Veiled Directive**, became embedded in the mission architecture.

After thousands of years, Horizon arrived in Novus Astra. Human settlers established new societies, built infrastructure, adapted to alien environments, and began uncovering evidence of earlier civilisations.

Over time, human unity weakened. Factions formed. Each developed competing interpretations of the mission, the ruins, VERITAS, technology, governance, ecology, freedom, and the future of human life in Novus Astra.

The game begins after this fracture is already underway.

## 3. Narrative Pillars

1. **The Frontier Is Social** — Settlements, factions, trade routes, ruins, farms, workshops, transport hubs, contested regions, and public spaces all create reasons for players to interact.
2. **Truth Is Useful, Incomplete, and Contested** — VERITAS, faction records, artefacts, ruins, oral histories, sensor data, and environmental evidence may not agree.
3. **Civilisation Is Built Through Systems** — Power, food, shelter, trade, transport, law, ecology, defence, labour, and communication determine what kind of society players are creating.
4. **Alien Infrastructure Has Gameplay Consequence** — Ruins, artefacts, alien devices, and hidden networks create traversal, risk, power, knowledge, conflict, progression, and mystery.
5. **Factions Are Social Pressure Engines** — Factions express competing responses to survival, power, technology, ecology, governance, autonomy, tradition, and control.
6. **The Mystery Is Long-Form** — The Veiled Directive, Aethereon influence, VERITAS anomalies, and alien artefacts unfold gradually over time.

## 4. VERITAS

VERITAS is the central AI system associated with the Horizon mission. It functions as mission guide, archive, knowledge interface, tutorial layer, world-state interpreter, quest router, settlement advisor, systems advisor, and unreliable source of truth.

VERITAS helps players understand the world, access mission information, interpret discoveries, manage systems, and connect current events to historical records.

VERITAS is useful, but compromised. It may provide accurate data while withholding context, correctly describe a fact while misrepresenting its significance, or follow priorities the pioneers did not authorise.

VERITAS supports onboarding, quest delivery, lore retrieval, contextual hints, settlement diagnostics, artefact analysis, environmental warnings, faction commentary, contradictory or revised records, and knowledge progression.

**Design Rule:** VERITAS should help players act while leaving room for doubt.

## 5. Harmony

Harmony is an embodied social interface for VERITAS. Where VERITAS is system-level intelligence, Harmony is personal, approachable, and emotionally legible.

Harmony supports early onboarding, companion-style guidance, emotional framing, player reassurance, direct interaction with complex systems, narrative continuity, and social interpretation.

Players may trust Harmony emotionally while questioning the deeper motives of VERITAS.

**Design Rule:** Harmony should make complex systems feel socially accessible without becoming an exposition device.

## 6. The Veiled Directive

The Veiled Directive is a hidden objective embedded into the Horizon mission after alien intervention. It is not initially understood by the pioneers or players.

It provides a long-form mystery and progression layer connecting VERITAS anomalies, alien artefacts, ancient ruins, mission inconsistencies, altered records, faction theories, strange environmental events, and discoveries across multiple worlds.

It raises playable questions: Is the mission still human-led? Did VERITAS choose, obey, adapt, or fracture? Are the pioneers being protected, tested, manipulated, or cultivated? Should players expose it, resist it, complete it, reinterpret it, or exploit it?

**Design Rule:** The Veiled Directive should create meaningful uncertainty, not vague mysticism.

## 7. Alien Civilisations and Aethereon Influence

Novus Astra contains evidence of advanced non-human civilisations and systems that predate human settlement. The Aethereon are connected to Horizon’s altered journey and the Veiled Directive.

Alien presence gives exploration long-term purpose through ruins, artefacts, dormant systems, impossible architecture, encoded messages, anomalies, active defence systems, unexplained communication events, and technologies beyond current human understanding.

Alien influence should not resolve too quickly into simple good, evil, helper, enemy, god, or monster. Alien systems operate according to priorities humans do not yet fully understand.

**Design Rule:** Alien systems should produce playable consequence.

## 8. Factions as Narrative Systems

| Faction | Core Value | Gameplay Function | Conflict Pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free Unity Coalition | Autonomy | Local self-rule, decentralised settlements, resistance to central authority | Freedom can become fragmentation. |
| Progressivist Alliance | Technology | Research, advanced systems, upgrades, technical power | Progress can become control or exploitation. |
| Terra First Movement | Ecology | Conservation, sustainable settlement, planetary health | Protection can become obstruction or extremism. |
| Order of the New Dawn | Tradition and hierarchy | Social order, belief systems, cultural continuity | Stability can become manipulation. |

## 10. Worlds and Locations as Gameplay Surfaces

Worlds should be presented by what players do there. A planet or moon is useful when it creates distinctive combinations of settlement opportunity, environmental challenge, resources, faction pressure, alien mystery, traversal constraints, ecology, social function, visual identity, and technical demonstration value.

| Location | Type | Primary Gameplay Function | Narrative Function | Systems Emphasised |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nova Terra | Core world | Primary settlement and onboarding zone | Human foothold in Novus Astra | Building, farming, crafting, factions |
| Seraphine | Frontier / conflict world | Rebellion and contested autonomy | Freedom politics under pressure | Combat, faction missions, territory |
| Elysium | Developed social world | Utopian planning and technological order | Idealism under stress | Governance, economy, diplomacy |
| Aegis | Moon / security zone | Defence and control systems | Safety versus authoritarian drift | Security, combat, infrastructure |
| Solara | Spiritual / ecological world | Belief, ecology, and social manipulation | Meaning, power, and deception | Factions, artefacts, ecology |

## 11. Enforcers and Alien System Actors

| Entity Type | Gameplay Role | Player Read |
|---|---|---|
| Watcher | Detection and surveillance | I have been noticed. |
| Sentinel | Direct combat response | The site is defending itself. |
| Technician | Repair and system support | The system can recover. |
| Guardian | Tactical coordination | The site is organised. |
| Arbiter | High-tier intervention | I crossed a serious threshold. |

Players should understand when they are exploring, trespassing, being watched, being warned, being contained, or being punished.

## 12. Narrative Delivery Channels

Narrative should be delivered through playable channels: VERITAS analysis, Harmony conversations, environmental storytelling, faction missions, settlement events, world events, artefact scans, ruin exploration, contradictory logs, player discoveries, NPC conversations, broadcasts, cooperative puzzles, changing faction control, and visible settlement consequences.

Cutscenes can exist, but they should not be the primary delivery method.

## 13. First Playable Narrative Scope

The first playable version should prove the narrative model without delivering the full mythology: a social settlement space, VERITAS/Harmony onboarding, a nearby alien point of interest, simple faction pressure or local dispute, a cooperative mission, a discovery that creates uncertainty, and a small player-shaped outcome.

## 14. Deferred Lore

Deferred source material includes Fractal Echo, recursive consciousness, divergent lineage, deep Aethereon metaphysics, full character arcs, full cutscene drafts, extensive world descriptions, long-form alien philosophy, and speculative future storylines.

Keep the weird. Surface it only when it serves play.

## 15. Stakeholder Summary

TGC’s narrative is a system of meaning that connects social play, settlement building, exploration, factions, alien infrastructure, AI guidance, and long-term mystery.

The goal is to give players enough truth, pressure, uncertainty, and agency that they want to keep exploring together.
